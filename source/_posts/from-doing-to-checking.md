This week, I worked on a proposal to change how a team handles a manual process. The technical part is already done. We built a solution with AI and a rules engine, and it works. However, the adoption rate is still low. So my proposal is not about technology. It is about changing the whole user process and helping people adopt a new way of working. For me, this is the hardest part.

Today, a person reads the documents, checks them against the rules, decides the outcome, and types everything into the system. With the new approach, AI reads the documents, checks them against the rules, and prepares a suggested decision. A person still reviews it and makes the final call. The work becomes faster and more consistent, but the job itself changes. People move from doing the work to checking the work.

Firstly, I learned that the technical part is not the hardest part. The hardest part is changing how people work. It takes many discussions and agreements, and every team member has to adopt a new way of working.

Secondly, I learned that fear often comes from not knowing. People know the new approach is good, but they are afraid that AI will replace their jobs. I think this is because many people do not really understand what AI can do and what it cannot do. AI can prepare a suggestion quickly, but the business still needs people to verify it and make the final decision.

>People don't resist change. They resist being changed. - Peter Senge

Next time, I will spend more time explaining what AI can and cannot do before I talk about the solution. When people understand the tool, they can trust it.

AI does not replace people. It changes what people spend their time on.
