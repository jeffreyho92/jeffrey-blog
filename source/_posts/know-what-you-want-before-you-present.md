Early this week, I gave a presentation to senior management. I shared a few problems I found in a team's process, a suggestion for each one, and how we could carry out the changes together. I prepared a lot of content. But during the meeting, I learned that more content is not always better.

Firstly, keep it short. Managers are very busy. When a point takes too long, they start to check their phones or laptops. So I try to keep the whole presentation within 15 minutes, and every point must be simple and direct. It is a bit like a trailer for a movie. It shows only the best parts, and people decide quickly whether they want to know more.

Secondly, different people care about different things. In the same room, each person listens for the problem that matters to them. If a point does not interest them, they wait for the next one. Therefore, I need to know my audience and connect each point to what they care about.

Thirdly, and this is the biggest lesson for me, I need to know what I want from the presentation. For example, if I am asking for resources, my request must be clear, so management can understand it and agree to it. Instead of focusing on the problems I want to show, I should focus on what I want to walk out with.

>Begin with the end in mind. - Stephen Covey

Next time, before I make any slides, I will write down one sentence: what decision do I want from this meeting? Then every slide should help me get that decision.

A good presentation is not about how much you say. It is about what you get.
