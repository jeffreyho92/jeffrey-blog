Last week, my colleagues helped me review a project update for senior management. I had used AI to gather information from my project, and my slides were full of technical detail. After our discussion, I realised I did not have a clear story yet.

I had prepared the presentation as an engineer. I explained how the system works, step by step. However, a lot of that was hard to follow for people who are not technical. My colleagues helped me find the real questions: what problem are we solving, and what does management need to know about it?

Firstly, I learned to adapt the content to the audience. It is a bit like giving directions. A friend who knows the area only needs the street name. A visitor needs the landmarks. In a technical discussion, I might explain how the system makes decisions. In a management update, I should start with the business problem and the improvement we expect. Technical results can then support that story.

Secondly, I learned that gathering information and preparing a presentation are two different jobs. AI helped me collect the material quickly, but it could not decide what belongs in the slides. More information did not make the choice easier. In fact, it made it harder.

>I have made this longer than usual because I have not had time to make it shorter. - Blaise Pascal

Next time, I will write a short explanation of the business problem before I build any slides. Then I will explain how our approach could help, and keep the expected benefits separate from the results we have already measured. Every technical detail should help the audience understand that story, or it stays out.

Collect widely, then choose carefully.
