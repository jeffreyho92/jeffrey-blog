This week, I presented a project update to senior management. As an engineer, I am used to explaining how things work. However, I noticed again that management looks at everything from a business angle. They only want to know one thing: will this increase revenue or reduce cost?

My project uses AI to help a team make routine decisions faster. People still review every AI decision before it is final. The value is simple. Work moves faster, the team becomes more productive, and the business can use faster service as an advantage.

Firstly, I learned to lead with business impact. It is a bit like selling a car. The buyer does not want to hear about every part of the engine. They want to know how it will make their life better. Technical results are still important, but they should support the story, not become the story.

>People don't buy what you do; they buy why you do it. - Simon Sinek

Secondly, I learned that good technology is not enough. A tool only creates value when people actually use it. Our adoption is still small, so the next step is to roll it out more widely, and keep humans in the loop to catch and fix mistakes. In other words, the biggest challenge is often not the system. It is helping people trust it and change the way they work.

Next time I present, I will start with the business problem and the impact first, and keep the technical details as support.

Build the right thing, then help people use it.
